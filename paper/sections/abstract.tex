\begin{abstract}
A decision model picks one answer from a set of options in a single forward pass, without writing out its reasoning.
It is cheap enough to run on every item in a large pipeline, for example to grade, filter or route text.
Small decoder models trained for this job are not very accurate, and we ask whether a small encoder is a better starting point.
We fine-tune UltraBERT-380M \citep{ultrabert}, an encoder pretrained with masked language modelling (5\% masking) and retrieval (50\% masking).
We train it with a sigmoid-normalised loss, where each option's sigmoid score is divided by the sum over options \citep{deepseekv3}.
We tested extensively on sub-billion encoder and decoder models.
Against Qwen3-0.6B trained on the same data with the LLM-as-Jev loss \citep{li2026llmasjev}, the encoder reaches
\textbf{\TestUbSig{}\%} test accuracy against \textbf{\TestQwFull{}\%}, averaged over seeds, and takes \textbf{\MsUb{}\,ms} per decision against \textbf{\MsQwFull{}\,ms} on an A100.
It also scores higher on external benchmarks (\textbf{\ExtUbSig{}\%} against \textbf{\ExtQwFull{}\%}).
The same encoder, with an added evidence head, marks the sentences that support its answer in one pass.
We train it on human-marked evidence and on citations generated with the SelfCite necessity and sufficiency reward \citep{selfcite}. Its sentences agree more closely with human-marked evidence than those from ContextCite or AT2.
\end{abstract}
